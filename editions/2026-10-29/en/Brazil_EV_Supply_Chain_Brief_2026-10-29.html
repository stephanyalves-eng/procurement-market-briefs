Brazil EV supply chain brief · Automotive sourcing · Edition 29 OCT 2026

Electrified car imports rose 160% to 425k units, 93% from China, and spiked before every July tariff step. From 2027, localization is the only cost lever left

Edition
29 Oct 2026

Brazil imported 425k battery-electric, plug-in and hybrid cars in Jan–Sep 2026 (195k BEV, 164k PHEV, 65k HEV), against 163k a year earlier. 50% of the year's volume landed in May and June, ahead of the July step that took all electrified categories to 35%. Average FOB value fell from USD 31.5k in 2023 to 16.5k per unit, consistent with kits for local assembly and cheaper models entering the mix. With kit duties converging to 35% in January 2027, the remaining lever is local content: batteries, body parts and tires.

Implications for sourcing

  1. Localize by duty saved, not by part count: at 35%, every USD 1,000 of imported kit content carries about USD 350 of import duty before other taxes. Rank parts by duty saved per tooling dollar; bumpers, tires, seats, glass and harnesses usually come first.
  2. Batteries are the largest block: Li-ion imports grew 66% with 84% from China. A staged path (imported cells, local pack assembly, then local modules) captures value while cell supply stays concentrated.
  3. Build the local supplier pipeline now: China went from 16% to 25% of auto parts imports in one year. PPAP and validation lead times mean 2027 nationalization targets need supplier nominations in the coming months.
  4. Smooth the inbound plan: no step remains after January 2027, so the May–June peaks that drove inventory and working capital can end. Renegotiate freight and storage on flatter volumes.
  5. Price FX into imported content: USD/BRL jumped 9% in one session on 26 Oct. Use FX adjustment clauses or hedging for USD and CNY content.
